> **Editorial rule:** Start with the deal. Understand each business separately. Connect the economics. Ask why ownership is necessary. Test the value and beneficiaries. A good strategy is not necessarily a good price.

## 01 / The Deal — What happened?
- Opening: announcement date, parties, reason reported, status.
- Transaction: value with correct EV/equity label, cash/share exchange, premium/reference date, conditions, anticipated closing.
- The question driving the story: why was this target worth buying?
- **Evidence:** Exact original filing + date + link.
- **Counterargument:** What could a headline number mislead readers about?
- **Cui bono:** Who receives consideration and who bears closing risk?

## 02 / The Acquirer — The buyer's business
- How does it make money? Customers? Product mix? Margins? Cash flow?
- Which strengths and strategic gaps matter to the deal?
- What changes in industry demand, competition or technology?
- What happens if the buyer does *nothing*?
- **Evidence:** Annual report and quarterly results; metrics with dates.
- **Counterargument:** Could the alleged strategic problem be exaggerated?
- **Cui bono:** How could current shareholders or customers benefit?

## 03 / The Target — The asset being bought
- How does it earn revenue? What is defensible? Key clients? Competitors?
- Assets: IP, customer access, data, talent, distribution, cash flow.
- What is difficult to build or buy elsewhere?
- **Evidence:** Target filings, contracts, disclosed unit economics and competitive data.
- **Counterargument:** What weakness does management not emphasize?
- **Cui bono:** What does the selling shareholder receive and retain?

## 06 / The Economics — Price, synergies & execution
- **Price:** Distinguish *equity purchase price* from *enterprise value*.
- **Financing:** Cash, stock, debt, dilution, earn-outs, approvals.
- **Valuation:** Standalone value + risk-adjusted NPV of **incremental** after-tax synergies − integration and financing impacts. Compare with total consideration; avoid double counting.
- **Claims vs outcomes:** Attribute management forecasts as forecasts.
- **Scenarios:** Base / bull / bear with key assumptions (not invented historical numbers).
- **Evidence:** Filings and historical accounts.
- **Counterargument:** What if synergies are late, costs higher, customers defect, or return on capital disappoints?
- **Cui bono:** How is economic value divided between buyer and seller?

## 07 / Cui Bono — Independent verdict
- Buyer shareholders: expected incremental return, dilution and risk.
- Seller shareholders: cash, stock, premium, residual exposure.
- Management: incentives; do **not** imply misconduct without evidence.
- Employees, customers, competitors, regulators: plausible effects and trade-offs.

- **Big idea:** A generalizable insight from the case.
